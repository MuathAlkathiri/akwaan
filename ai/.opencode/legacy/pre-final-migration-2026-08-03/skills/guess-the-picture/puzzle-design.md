# Puzzle Design Rules

## Purpose

Design high-quality Arabic rebus puzzles where players combine the spoken names of images to discover a completely new Arabic word or expression.

The goal is not to describe the images.
The goal is to transform their spoken names into a new answer.

---

# Design Philosophy

A puzzle should create an "Aha!" moment.

Players should feel rewarded when they discover how the image names combine into the final answer.

If the answer is already obvious by simply describing the images, the puzzle has failed.

---

# Answer-First Workflow

Always begin with the final answer.

Never begin with random images and attempt to invent an answer afterward.

Workflow:

1. Select a candidate Arabic answer.
2. Determine whether the answer can be naturally decomposed into spoken Arabic parts.
3. If no natural decomposition exists, reject the answer immediately.
4. Find one clear visual concept for every spoken part.
5. Verify the spoken composition.
6. Validate the puzzle.
7. Generate images only after all validation passes.

Never attempt to "force" a decomposition.

If a candidate cannot be decomposed naturally, discard it and choose another answer.

---

# Supported Puzzle Types

## Exact Phonetic

The spoken names of the images exactly form the final answer.

Example:

سم + بوسة = سمبوسة

---

## Natural Phonetic

A very small and natural pronunciation blend is acceptable if it reflects normal spoken Arabic.

The pronunciation must remain immediately understandable to a native Arabic speaker.

Do not invent missing sounds.
Do not remove meaningful sounds.
Do not rearrange the spoken order.

---

# Rebus Rule

Every accepted puzzle must produce a NEW answer.

The answer must never be the literal description of the displayed images.

The images are clues.

The answer is the result of combining their spoken names.

---

# Semantic Surprise Rule

A successful puzzle contains semantic surprise.

The player should not solve the puzzle simply by naming what they see.

Instead, the spoken image names should combine into a different Arabic word or expression.

Example:

سم + بوسة = سمبوسة

The answer "سمبوسة" is a completely new word.
It is not a literal description of poison and a kiss.

---

# Invalid Puzzle Patterns

Reject immediately if the answer is simply:

- object + object
- adjective + noun
- noun + body part
- noun + location
- noun + material
- familiar phrase directly represented by the images
- any literal visual description

Examples:

❌ حقيبة + يد = حقيبة يد

❌ فنجان + قهوة = فنجان قهوة

❌ ساعة + حائط = ساعة حائط

❌ قلم + حبر = قلم حبر

❌ عيد + ميلاد + سعيد = عيد ميلاد سعيد

These are literal image descriptions, not rebus puzzles.

---

# Accessibility calibration

## Easy

- two spoken parts
- exact pronunciation
- common objects
- almost zero ambiguity

---

## Medium

- two or three spoken parts
- slight natural pronunciation blending
- requires a small amount of reasoning
- still immediately fair

---

## Hard

- clever Arabic decomposition
- culturally familiar answer
- fair but challenging
- still fully solvable without hints

Hard must never mean:

- random
- obscure
- unfair
- linguistically incorrect
- visually confusing

---

# Candidate Quality

Prefer answers that:

- are commonly understood in Modern Standard Arabic or Gulf Arabic
- create an enjoyable "Aha!" moment
- can be represented by clear visual concepts
- have one intended solution
- remain understandable without captions
- require minimal pronunciation assumptions

Avoid answers that:

- require individual letters
- require spelling instead of pronunciation
- depend on obscure vocabulary
- require multiple hidden sound changes
- require explaining the joke
- have no natural visual decomposition

---

# Hard Rejection Rule

Immediately reject a candidate when:

- no natural spoken decomposition exists
- the decomposition requires explanation
- the answer is simply the image names placed together
- the answer literally describes the displayed images
- the puzzle contains little or no semantic surprise
- multiple different answers are equally reasonable
- the puzzle feels forced

When rejected:

Do not attempt to repair the puzzle.

Discard the candidate completely and choose a different answer.
