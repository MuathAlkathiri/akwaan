# Content Scope Knowledge: Anime / One Piece

## Scope Purpose

This file provides durable boundaries, naming guidance, and coverage structure
for One Piece content. It is not an encyclopedia and does not replace research.
Use it with the Anime World Skill, the selected Anime Challenge Skill, and the
batch spoiler policy.

Default scope: the One Piece television anime and stable underlying canon that
the anime has already revealed. Manga-only material, films, specials, games,
novels, live action, promotional crossovers, and filler are excluded unless a
manifest explicitly includes them.

## Stable Knowledge Foundation

The durable premise follows Monkey D. Luffy and the Straw Hat Pirates on a
voyage through a world dominated by oceans, islands, pirates, the Marines, and
the World Government, in pursuit of the treasure known as One Piece and the
title associated with the Pirate King.

Stable world concepts suitable for orientation include:

- the Grand Line, Red Line, Calm Belts, and the role of unusual navigation;
- Log Poses and island-to-island travel;
- Devil Fruits and their broad place in the world;
- Haki as a major power system once revealed by the anime;
- pirate crews, bounties, the Marines, and the World Government;
- Poneglyphs and the historical mysteries around them;
- the Void Century, Ancient Weapons, and the Will of D. only at the level
  already disclosed by the allowed spoiler tier;
- the Four Emperors and the former Seven Warlords system with explicit temporal
  context;
- inherited will, dreams, promises, freedom, and found family as recurring
  thematic ideas.

The exact mechanics, classifications, memberships, and current status of these
concepts may evolve. Durable presence does not make every detail permanently
stable.

## Major Eras and Arcs

Use arc names as coverage anchors, not as a rigid universal taxonomy. Official
publishers and fan communities may group arcs into sagas differently.

Useful high-level progression:

1. East Blue;
2. Alabasta-related journey;
3. Sky Island;
4. Water 7 and Enies Lobby;
5. Thriller Bark;
6. Sabaody, Amazon Lily, Impel Down, and Marineford / Summit War period;
7. post-war transition and time skip;
8. Fish-Man Island;
9. Punk Hazard and Dressrosa;
10. Zou and Whole Cake Island;
11. Wano Country;
12. Egghead;
13. Elbaph, currently evolving and requiring fresh verification.

Do not assume every audience uses the same saga grouping or transliteration.
When chronology is the question, use unambiguous named arcs and verify the
continuity allowed by the manifest.

## Important Organizations, Crews, and Factions

Stable coverage may draw from:

- Straw Hat Pirates;
- Marines;
- World Government;
- Revolutionary Army;
- Four Emperors and their crews, with time context;
- Seven Warlords of the Sea as a historical institution, with time context;
- Cipher Pol agencies;
- Worst Generation;
- Straw Hat Grand Fleet;
- major arc-specific kingdoms, crews, alliances, and resistance groups.

Organizations whose status or membership changes must be bound to a clear era.
Cross Guild, Rocks-related history, current Emperor alignment, and current
Government or Revolutionary developments require fresh verification and careful
spoiler labeling.

## Major Locations

Useful stable location coverage includes East Blue islands, Reverse Mountain,
the Grand Line, Alabasta, Skypiea, Water 7, Enies Lobby, Thriller Bark,
Sabaody Archipelago, Impel Down, Marineford, Fish-Man Island, Punk Hazard,
Dressrosa, Zou, Whole Cake Island, Wano Country, and Egghead.

Location identity from an image or map belongs to the Places Challenge. Otaku
may use a location when the core fun is knowledge about its role, history,
institution, or place in an unambiguous chronology.

## High-Level Chronology Rules

- distinguish pre–time skip and post–time skip material;
- state the relevant era for changing crews, titles, bounties, alliances, or
  institutions;
- do not use exact elapsed time unless an authoritative source makes it clear;
- do not infer chronology solely from episode order when flashbacks are involved;
- separate historical events inside the story from the present voyage;
- avoid ranking revelations by recency without fresh verification.

## Spoiler Tiers

### Tier A — Broad premise

Premise, early crew identity, widely established world concepts, and early arcs.
Suitable for broadly accessible batches.

### Tier B — Established anime history

Major developments through older completed anime arcs. The manifest should name
the allowed endpoint or minimum cooling period.

### Tier C — Recent anime

Material from recently aired episodes or the current anime arc. Requires fresh
verification and an explicit recent-anime permission.

### Tier D — Manga-only or unreleased adaptation material

Excluded by default. Requires explicit manga permission, current chapter
verification, and prominent spoiler control across research, drafts,
explanations, and media.

Current source checks indicate the anime has entered Elbaph in 2026. Treat all
Elbaph status, episode boundaries, and adaptation details as evolving rather
than durable knowledge.

## Translation-Sensitive or Disputed Terminology

Verify before use:

- `Zoro` versus edition-specific `Zolo`;
- `Jinbe` / `Jimbei` and Arabic `جينبي` / `جيمبي`;
- `Mary Geoise` / `Mariejois` and Arabic renderings;
- `Levely` / `Reverie`;
- `Laugh Tale` and older renderings;
- `Poneglyph` transliterations;
- `Seven Warlords`, `Shichibukai`, and Arabic descriptive forms;
- epithets, attack names, island names, and translated institutional titles.

Do not make spelling disagreement the source of difficulty. Store verified
aliases when the concept is identical. Treat materially different translations
as a review warning, not an opportunity to guess.

## Current or Evolving Canon Requiring Fresh Verification

Always verify live before research handoff:

- the latest anime arc, episode, and release status;
- manga-only revelations and whether the anime has adapted them;
- current crew, alliance, Emperor, Government, or bounty status;
- unresolved mysteries and newly explained world history;
- living/deceased/unknown status after recent events;
- current official romanization or localization changes;
- hiatuses, broadcast changes, and regional availability;
- statements attributed to Eiichiro Oda outside the primary work.

Never turn fan theories, leaks, unofficial translations, or promotional
summaries into settled canon.

## Anime-Only, Manga-Only, and Non-Default Material

- Anime-only: filler arcs, anime-original scenes, recaps, altered pacing, and
  adaptation-specific dialogue must be labeled and included only when allowed.
- Manga-only: excluded from anime-safe batches until adapted and inside the
  manifest boundary.
- Films and specials: separate continuity unless explicitly scoped.
- SBS material and databooks: potentially useful for Otaku, but verify edition,
  translation, and whether the reveal is genuinely enjoyable.
- Live action and games: separate adaptations, excluded by default.

## Otaku-Suitable Content Areas

- meaningful organization purposes and historical roles;
- stable crew or faction relationships with time context;
- consequences of major events;
- broad world rules and institutions;
- clear arc progression;
- established titles, promises, alliances, and political structures;
- thematic or creator knowledge when authoritative and socially interesting;
- distinctions fans commonly confuse but official material resolves.

1. primary manga chapters and official licensed manga publication;
2. official anime episodes and Toei Animation material;

4. official licensed distributors for regional release status;
5. authoritative editorial or reference sources for navigation only;

   terminology issue, followed by primary verification.

- `https://www.toei-animation.com/catalog/one-piece/` — official anime overview;
- current first-party Toei announcements for anime arc status.

Search snippets and unsourced wiki claims are discovery aids, not final proof.

## Uncertainty Rule

If continuity, translation, release state, chronology, or canon status is not
clear, label it for fresh research or exclude it. Durable Scope knowledge should
help agents know what to verify; it must never create confidence that the source
material does not support.
