# Challenge Skill: Anime / Voices

## Authority

Follow `.opencode/knowledge/AKWAN-CONTENT-BIBLE.md` and
`.opencode/skills/worlds/anime/SKILL.md`. Use the selected Anime Content Scope
for cast, canon, and naming. Route overlaps with
`../CHALLENGE-BOUNDARIES.md`.

## 1. Challenge Identity

Voices is a listening Challenge built around spoken, laughed, shouted, or
otherwise nonmusical vocal performance. The sound of the performance must be
the primary clue.

## 2. Intended Player Experience

Players listen closely, recognize a familiar vocal presence, and share the
delight of hearing a memorable character or performer without visual help.

## 3. Core Source of Fun

Auditory recognition of timbre, rhythm, delivery, laugh, catchphrase cadence,
or performance style—not reading a quote or seeing its speaker.

## 4. Accepted Content

- clear character voices, laughs, cries, vocal quirks, and spoken phrases;
- performer recognition when explicitly permitted and broadly enjoyable;
- comparisons of genuinely distinguishable vocal performances;
- short vocal moments with enough clean evidence for the target audience.

## 5. Rejected Content

- openings, songs, score, sound effects, or visual-only prompts;
- written quotes with no audio-recognition mechanic;
- audio that speaks the intended answer or depends on subtitles;
- noisy, distorted, excessively short, generic, or obscure performance clips.

## 6. Boundaries With Neighboring Challenges

- **Characters:** visual identity belongs there; audio-only speaker identity is
  Voices.
- **Otaku:** cast facts or quote knowledge belong there when listening is not
  required.
- **Abilities:** named activations belong there only when the power sound, not
  speaker identity, is central.
- **Scenes:** dialogue used to identify the event belongs to Scenes; isolate a
  vocal performance here.
- **Openings:** musical opening recognition always belongs to Openings.
- **Places:** ambience-led location recognition belongs to Places.
- **Symbols and Objects:** device or object sounds belong there only when object
  identity, rather than a human vocal performance, is central.

## 7. Supported Question Formats

Blind voice recognition, laugh recognition, paired performance distinction,
same-character performance comparison, and progressive audio reveal. Formats
must remain listening-led.

## 8. Suitable Media

Short clean audio or video with visuals hidden or neutralized. Retain enough
lead-in and vocal texture, normalize intelligibility, and document exact clip
boundaries without altering identity-defining sound.

## 9. Leakage Risks

The answer may be spoken, subtitled, captioned, labeled in player controls,
embedded in metadata, announced by another character, or exposed by a visible
speaker. Background music may disclose an opening or scene instead.

## 10. Answer Requirements

Specify whether the answer is character, performer, or another permitted vocal
identity; never accept these interchangeably. Record canonical Arabic names and
verified aliases. The clip must support one reasonable answer.

## 11. Arabic and Naming Rules

Use natural Arabic prompts and consistent character names. For performer names,
verify Arabic spelling and romanization. Do not translate a vocal catchphrase
in a way that inserts the answer.

## 12. Spoiler Policy

Voices can reveal identities, returns, allegiances, deaths, or later forms
through dialogue and context. Trim content to the manifest tier, including
background speech and captions.

## 13. Variety Expectations

Vary speakers, genders, ages, moods, laugh versus speech, intensity, and clip
shape across Scopes. Avoid repeated catchphrases, impressions, or one dominant
series and performer.

## 14. Common Failure Modes

Answer spoken aloud; clip recognized only from dialogue facts; music dominates;
visual speaker visible; poor mix; common scream with no identity; clip too short
to be fair; and performer/character answer confusion.

## 15. Writer Checklist

- Is listening indispensable and is vocal identity the fun?
- Is the requested answer type explicit and singular?
- Are clip limits, language, spoilers, and forbidden spoken leaks documented?

## 16. Reviewer Checklist

- Listen without subtitles or metadata and verify independent recognizability.
- Route music, event, ability, character-visual, lore, place, or object tasks.
- Reject generic, noisy, answer-speaking, or overly obscure clips.

## 17. Asset Curator Checklist

- Perform a blind listen with filename, description, and visuals hidden.
- Check the entire clip for spoken, captioned, and contextual leaks.
- Record clean timestamps and reject edits that distort the vocal signature.

## 18. QA Checklist

- Test on expected devices at normal volume and actual clip length.
- Confirm answer type, aliases, tier, evidence, leakage, and source traceability.
- Check auditory variety and block duplicates or unusable audio.

## 19. Good Conceptual Examples

Recognizing an iconic laugh; distinguishing two familiar vocal deliveries;
identifying a character from a clean non-revealing spoken moment. These are
directions, not production questions.

## 20. Weak or Misplaced Conceptual Examples

Reading a famous quote; hearing a song; a clip that says the character's name;
identifying a battle from its dialogue; or a generic shout under loud music.
