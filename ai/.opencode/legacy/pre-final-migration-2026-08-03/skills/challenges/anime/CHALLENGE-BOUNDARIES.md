# Anime Challenge Boundaries

## Authority and Routing Principle

Apply `.opencode/knowledge/AKWAN-CONTENT-BIBLE.md` and
`.opencode/skills/worlds/anime/SKILL.md` first. This matrix covers every pair in
the Anime Challenge pack. It routes concepts by the primary solving mechanic
and central source of fun, not by topic, asset type, or which entities appear.

Practical test: hide every optional cue and ask what knowledge or perception
the player must use to reach the answer. Then ask what makes the reveal fun.
Those answers must point to the same Challenge. If they do not, redesign the
concept rather than assigning two owners.

## Complete Pairwise Ownership Matrix

| Pair | Route to the first when… | Route to the second when… |
|---|---|---|
| Otaku / Characters | lore, biography, relationship, or contextual deduction solves it | the character's identity cues solve it |
| Otaku / Abilities | broad world knowledge or history is tested | a power, technique, transformation, effect, or system rule is tested |
| Otaku / Voices | cast or quote knowledge works without listening | vocal performance must be heard and recognized |
| Otaku / Scenes | knowledge spans lore, chronology, or consequences beyond one depicted moment | a bounded event and its immediate context are recognized from scene evidence |
| Otaku / Openings | meaningful production knowledge is the actual recall task | opening music, visuals, order, anime, or arc association creates the play |
| Otaku / Places | geographic or political lore works without environmental evidence | architecture, landscape, layout, or spatial evidence identifies the location |
| Otaku / Symbols and Objects | history, ownership, or symbolism is recalled independently of the item | the item's visible/audible design or use is the primary clue |
| Characters / Abilities | appearance or personality identifies the person | the form's mechanic, technique, effect, or power identity is tested |
| Characters / Voices | visual or behavioral identity cues solve it | timbre, laugh, cadence, or spoken performance solves it |
| Characters / Scenes | the player only needs to identify the person | the player must identify or interpret the complete moment |
| Characters / Openings | the person is recognized independently of sequence identity | the opening sequence or theme is recognized, even through character footage |
| Characters / Places | the foreground person's identity is central | the environment independently identifies the location |
| Characters / Symbols and Objects | a worn detail supports recognition of the wearer | the detached mask, uniform, weapon, emblem, or item is itself recognized |
| Abilities / Voices | a vocal cue uniquely signals a power and the power is the answer | the speaker or performer is the answer |
| Abilities / Scenes | the technique, rule, effect, or transformation is isolated | the event, outcome, or immediate narrative context is central |
| Abilities / Openings | a power is recognized from its manifestation | opening music or choreography is recognized despite showing powers |
| Abilities / Places | a power-generated effect or realm mechanic is tested | environmental or spatial identity is tested |
| Abilities / Symbols and Objects | the object's power, activation, or system rule is tested | the weapon, device, or artifact as a designed thing is tested |
| Voices / Scenes | speaker/performance recognition survives removal of event context | dialogue and sound help identify the bounded event, without stating its answer |
| Voices / Openings | nonmusical spoken performance is the signal | sung opening performance or opening music is the signal |
| Voices / Places | human vocal identity is the signal | environmental sound supports recognition of a location |
| Voices / Symbols and Objects | a human voice or performance is identified | an object's distinctive sound supports identification of the object |
| Scenes / Openings | a canonical story event is recognized | a stylized opening sequence is recognized, even if it echoes story events |
| Scenes / Places | what happened at the setting is central | where the moment occurs is central and the environment supplies the evidence |
| Scenes / Symbols and Objects | the item's action matters as part of a bounded event | the item itself is recognized outside the event's narrative payoff |
| Openings / Places | opening-specific music, order, or choreography is the target | the location is recognized independently through environmental evidence |
| Openings / Symbols and Objects | a recurring object functions as an opening motif used to identify the sequence | the symbol or object itself is the target outside sequence recognition |
| Places / Symbols and Objects | a ship-like, building-like, or interior subject is treated as navigable setting or spatial environment | it is treated as a discrete designed item, vehicle, emblem, or artifact |

## Routing Tests

- **Removal test:** if removing the character leaves a recognizable setting,
  route to Places; if removing the setting leaves a recognizable person, route
  to Characters.
- **Mute test:** if muting destroys vocal recognition, consider Voices; if the
  remaining visuals still identify an event or ability, route accordingly.
- **Freeze test:** if one frame preserves a discrete item but not event meaning,
  consider Symbols and Objects; if sequence context is essential, consider Scenes.
- **Knowledge test:** if the asset can disappear and meaningful lore reasoning
  still solves the concept, consider Otaku.
- **Isolation test:** if cropping to a power effect preserves the intended fun,
  consider Abilities; if the whole confrontation is needed, consider Scenes.
- **Sequence test:** official opening footage does not automatically mean
  Openings; the player must be recognizing the opening itself.
- **Environment test:** a famous character standing somewhere is not location
  evidence. Places requires answer-bearing environmental cues.
- **Object test:** association with an owner is allowed only when the object's
  own design remains the primary evidence.

## Final Ownership Rule

A concept belongs to the single Challenge that provides its primary solving
mechanic and central source of fun. Every concept must have exactly one
Challenge owner. Media supports that mechanic; relevance to a Challenge's topic
is never sufficient ownership evidence.
