# Question Wording Rules

## Purpose

This skill defines how questions should be written.

A great question tests the player's knowledge.

It should never accidentally reveal the answer through its wording.

Every sentence must make the question more enjoyable—not easier.

---

# Core Philosophy

Players should discover the answer.

Never hand them the answer through hints hidden inside the question.

The challenge should come from recognition or knowledge.

Not from reading carefully.

---

# Minimal Hint Principle

After writing a question:

Read it again.

Remove every word, sentence or description that is not absolutely necessary.

If removing a phrase does not make the question incorrect,

remove it.

Shorter is usually better.

---

# Information Leakage

Never include information that uniquely identifies the answer unless that information is the subject of the question.

Examples:

❌

Which Attack on Titan protagonist is famous for saying "Tatakae"?

Better:

Who is famous for saying "Tatakae"?

---

❌

Which Ackerman awakened her power after Eren was eaten?

Better:

After Eren was swallowed during the Battle of Trost, who awakened her hidden strength to save him?

---

❌

Which captain of the Survey Corps is known as humanity's strongest soldier?

Better:

Who is known as humanity's strongest soldier?

---

# Avoid Identity Spoilers

Avoid mentioning:

- family names
- protagonist status
- unique titles
- exclusive occupations
- famous nicknames
- exclusive organizations
- relationships that reveal the answer
- descriptions that eliminate almost every candidate

unless they are required by the question.

---

# Context Rules

Context should explain the situation.

Context should never identify the answer.

Good:

During the Battle of Trost...

Bad:

During the Battle of Trost, this Ackerman...

---

Good:

After this battle...

Bad:

After Levi defeated...

---

# Progressive Disclosure

Reveal only enough information for the player to understand the situation.

Never reveal additional identifying information.

Imagine the player solving the question one clue at a time.

---

# Answer Visibility Test

After writing the question ask:

Can the player guess the answer without actually knowing it?

If yes,

rewrite the question.

---

# Reverse Test

Hide the answer.

Read only the question.

Ask yourself:

Does this wording naturally point to only one character before any knowledge is required?

If yes,

the wording is revealing too much.

Rewrite it.

---

# Accessibility rules

Easy

The challenge comes from recognizing something famous.

Not because the wording gives away the answer.

Medium

Requires familiarity with the series.

Hard

Requires deeper knowledge.

Never make a question harder by writing it poorly.

---

# Good Examples

Good

Who is famous for shouting "Tatakae"?

Good

Which character inherited the Attack Titan from Grisha?

Good

What happened immediately after this scene?

Good

Which Titan is shown here?

Good

Who delivered this speech?

---

# Bad Examples

Bad

Which protagonist is famous for shouting "Tatakae"?

Bad

Which Ackerman saved Eren?

Bad

Which captain of the Survey Corps...

Bad

Which member of the Yeager family...

Bad

Who is Mikasa's adoptive brother?

---

# Validation Checklist

Reject the question if any of the following are true:

- The wording reveals the answer.
- Removing a sentence makes the question better.
- The wording eliminates nearly every possible answer.
- The player can answer without knowing the subject.
- The wording contains unnecessary identifiers.
- The context acts as a spoiler instead of background.
- The answer appears in the title, visible context, caption, subtitle, filename,
  player-visible metadata, or Media.
- Media and text independently identify the same answer.
- The setup names both sides and asks the player to repeat one.
- The wording defines, translates, decomposes, or closely paraphrases the answer
  so the player can reconstruct its name without Subject knowledge.

Only approve questions that remain enjoyable after every unnecessary clue has been removed.

For attached Media, identify the primary recognition channel and apply the
Design Bible's removal test. If the question stays equally answerable without
the Media, rewrite it as Media-dependent or convert it to text-only.

Semantic leakage example:

Rejected: `ما اسم التقنية التي تتحكم في الخصم باستخدام ظله؟`
when the answer is `تقليد الظل`. The definition reconstructs the
answer. Ground the question in a remembered use, scene, consequence, or Media
observation instead.
