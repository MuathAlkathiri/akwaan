# Exemplars: general-knowledge / science

Worked items for this Scope, authored for **اقرأ خصمك (Read Your Opponent)**.

## ⚠️ Every item below is a retired subject

They exist to show **which property makes an item work**. They are not
templates, and they are not a menu to choose from — every one has already been
authored and is unavailable. Do not reuse a subject, its wording or its options,
and do not reproduce one with a value changed. Read each for its *property*,
named in bold beneath it, then author something new that has that property.

---

## What RYO actually asks of a question

The opposing team does not answer. They decide **Steal or Trust** — a bet on
whether the answering team will get it right — in the same blind window
(roadmap §6.1). The payoff is asymmetric and the failed steal costs **−1**, so
the bet has to be genuinely hard to call.

That makes one property decisive, and it is not difficulty:

> **Would a reasonable opposing team have to think about Steal or Trust?**

A question everyone knows is answered correctly, so the opponent always Steals.
A question nobody knows is answered wrongly, so the opponent always Trusts.
Either way the mind game disappears and the round resolves itself. Aim for
roughly **40–80% confidence from the table's point of view** — the band where
the opposing team must actually read the room.

The second decisive property is **plausible alternatives**. Four options of
which three are obviously wrong is a 100%-certainty question wearing a costume:
the opponent can see the giveaway as easily as the answering team can.

Everything below also has to satisfy this Scope: recognizable science, no
academic equations, no obscure terminology, **no meaningless numeric
precision**.

## Worked examples — every one is a RETIRED subject

Each heading names the **property** the item demonstrates. That property is what
you reuse. The question, its answer and its options are spent.

### 1. The widely-held wrong intuition

**RETIRED — do not reuse this subject.** Reusable property: *a misconception most people hold is as common as the fact itself.*

> «ما الغاز الذي يشكّل النسبة الأكبر من الغلاف الجوي للأرض؟»
> النيتروجين · الأكسجين · ثاني أكسيد الكربون · بخار الماء

- Correct: **النيتروجين** (about 78%)
- `estimatedConfidence`: **even** — the misconception is as common as the fact.
- `distractorRationale`:
  - `الأكسجين` — the gas people associate with breathing, so it is the first
    reach for most tables.
  - `ثاني أكسيد الكربون` — carries the strongest everyday association with "what
    is in the air" through climate talk.
  - `بخار الماء` — genuinely present and variable, so a player who knows humidity
    exists can talk themselves into it.
- **The property:** most tables reach for الأكسجين first and some of them stop
  there. That split is exactly the 40–80% band — the answering team may well
  know, and the opponent genuinely cannot tell. Every distractor is a real
  atmospheric gas, so nothing is given away by elimination, and each one can be
  justified in a sentence. **That is the test for an option: if you cannot say
  why someone would pick it, replace it.**

### 2. Order-of-magnitude reasoning, not recall

**RETIRED — do not reuse this subject.** Reusable property: *options separated by scale, so reasoning beats recall.*

> «كم يستغرق ضوء الشمس حتى يصل إلى الأرض؟»
> ٨ ثوانٍ · ٨ دقائق · ٨ ساعات · ٨ أيام

- Correct: **٨ دقائق** (about 8 minutes 20 seconds)
- **The property:** a team that does not know can still reason — light is fast,
  but the Sun is very far — and land on the right scale. The options differ by
  orders of magnitude rather than by precision, which keeps it inside the
  Scope's ban on meaningless numeric precision while staying genuinely
  uncertain.

### 3. A familiar number that few people hold exactly

**RETIRED — do not reuse this subject.** Reusable property: *a figure everyone has heard and few can retrieve under pressure.*

> «كم عدد العظام في جسم الإنسان البالغ؟»
> ١٨٦ · ٢٠٦ · ٢٢٦ · ٢٤٦

- Correct: **٢٠٦**
- **The property:** almost everyone has heard the figure and many cannot
  retrieve it under ten seconds of pressure. The distractors sit close enough
  that elimination does not work, and the answer is fixed — anatomy does not
  drift the way a record or a statistic does.

### 4. Two things people routinely swap

**RETIRED — do not reuse this subject.** Reusable property: *two recognizable candidates with a genuine reason to confuse them.*

> «أي العالِمَين وضع قوانين الحركة الثلاثة؟»
> إسحاق نيوتن · ألبرت أينشتاين · غاليليو غاليلي · نيكولا تسلا

- Correct: **إسحاق نيوتن**
- **The property:** every option is a recognizable scientist, so the question is
  fair; but Galileo's work on motion makes him a real competitor rather than
  filler. A reviewer should ask whether the distractors earn their place — here
  they do.

## Rejected exemplars

| Prompt (abridged) | Why it fails RYO |
|---|---|
| «ما الكوكب الذي يُلقَّب بالكوكب الأحمر؟» | Certainty near 100%. The opponent steals every time and the decision is free. The question is not *wrong* — it is simply not an RYO question. |
| «من وضع نظرية النسبية؟» | Same defect. A correct, fair, recognizable question with no mind game in it. |
| «ما الكتلة الذرية للروثينيوم بثلاث منازل عشرية؟» | Certainty near 0%, and the Scope excludes meaningless numeric precision outright. The opponent simply trusts. |
| «كم عدد أقمار زحل؟» | The answer moves — confirmed moons rise with each announcement — so it cannot be graded a season later. |
| «ما أكبر كوكب؟ المشتري · الأرض · القمر · الشمس» | Three distractors are not plausible planets at all. Visible elimination collapses it back to certainty. |

The thread: each either removes the Steal/Trust decision or removes fairness.
A good RYO item keeps both alive at once.

## A note for review

Confidence is a judgement, not a measurement. The band above is a target for
authoring and a prompt for the reviewer — *would a table really have to think?*
— not a number any validator can check. The deterministic screen can confirm
option counts, unique ids, and that the answer does not leak into the prompt;
it cannot confirm that a question is interesting.
