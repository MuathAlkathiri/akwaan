# Difficulty Design

Difficulty comes from fair recognition or meaningful knowledge, not obscurity.

## Easy

Accessible to the intended audience, but never automatic. Use famous events,
clear scenes, familiar objects, or well-known abilities. Do not use the
protagonist or another obvious character as a Direct Character Identification
answer.

## Medium

Reward regular fans. Use supporting characters, less immediate scenes, event
connections, and details the audience encountered often enough to remember.

## Hard

Reward dedicated fans with brief but memorable moments, layered cause and
effect, sequence depth, less prominent yet meaningful characters, or specific
abilities. Hard must still be enjoyable and verifiable.

Never create difficulty with production trivia, episode or release numbers
unless the Catalog explicitly values them, ambiguity, missing context, low
resolution, unfair crops, trick wording, or multiple valid answers.

Preserve the project's established difficulty or points mapping, including
200/400/600 when that is the existing model.
