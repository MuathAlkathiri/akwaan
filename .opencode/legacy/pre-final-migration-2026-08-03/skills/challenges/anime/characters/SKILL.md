# Challenge Skill: Anime / Characters

## Authority

Follow `.opencode/knowledge/AKWAN-CONTENT-BIBLE.md` and
`.opencode/skills/worlds/anime/SKILL.md`. Use the selected Anime Content Scope
for canon and naming. Route overlaps with `../CHALLENGE-BOUNDARIES.md`.

## 1. Challenge Identity

Characters is recognition of an anime character through distinctive identity
cues. The character—not a fact, event, power, place, voice, or object—is the
thing the player must recognize.

## 2. Intended Player Experience

Players should enjoy the instant or gradual spark of recognizing a familiar
person from fair visual, behavioral, or constrained descriptive evidence.

## 3. Core Source of Fun

Recognition of personality and appearance. Vary the reveal: silhouette,
partial detail, expression, outfit era, visual progression, or combined cues;
do not reduce the Challenge to repeated portrait naming.

## 4. Accepted Content

- distinctive face, silhouette, hairstyle, costume, posture, or mannerism;
- character variants or eras when the shown form is canonically clear;
- identity deduction from several character-specific, non-biographical cues;
- comparisons whose primary action is distinguishing characters.

## 5. Rejected Content

- biography, relationships, affiliations, or lore as the main test;
- a voice-only identity task, complete event, power demonstration, location,
  or detached signature object;
- generic background figures, ambiguous transformations, or unreadable crops;
- evidence that depends on labels, captions, filenames, or hidden context.

## 6. Boundaries With Neighboring Challenges

- **Otaku:** route there when knowledge or relationships solve it; stay here
  when identity cues solve it.
- **Abilities:** route a power or transformation-mechanic recognition there;
  a transformed character stays here only when identity remains central.
- **Voices:** voice-only speaker recognition belongs to Voices.
- **Scenes:** a complete event or what-happened task belongs to Scenes.
- **Openings:** recognizing an opening sequence belongs to Openings, even when
  it features a character.
- **Places:** environmental identity belongs to Places.
- **Symbols and Objects:** a weapon, mask, emblem, or artifact detached from its
  wearer belongs there; a worn detail may support character identity here.

## 7. Supported Question Formats

Direct visual recognition, silhouette or crop reveal, progressive reveal,
before-and-after comparison, character distinction, and constrained identity
clues. Format serves recognition and never defines ownership by itself.

## 8. Suitable Media

Clean stills, portraits, silhouettes, short loops of mannerisms, or ordered
visual variants. The actual media must make the identity reasonably visible.

## 9. Leakage Risks

Names in subtitles, UI, watermarks, credits, alt text, filename, costume text,
or surrounding cast context can give away the answer. Crop only when the
remaining character evidence stays sufficient.

## 10. Answer Requirements

Use one canonical character identity, accepted Arabic spellings, verified
romanizations, and aliases only when the depicted era legitimately uses them.
Reject a crop shared by several plausible characters.

## 11. Arabic and Naming Rules

Use natural Arabic and the Anime World naming policy. Prefer the established
Arabic fandom form; record useful transliteration variants without accepting a
different person, title, or family name accidentally.

## 12. Spoiler Policy

Respect the manifest tier. A late costume, identity reveal, scar, form, alias,
or affiliation can itself be a spoiler; media and explanations must be safe at
the same tier as the prompt.

## 13. Variety Expectations

Vary cast prominence, eras, cue types, moods, compositions, and recognition
actions across selected Scopes. Avoid repeated frontal portraits or a batch
dominated by protagonists.

## 14. Common Failure Modes

Portrait-name monotony; tiny subjects; generic silhouettes; several equally
dominant characters; identity leaked by text; a biography disguised as clues;
and transformations routed by who appears rather than what players recognize.

## 15. Writer Checklist

- Is character identity the primary solving mechanic and source of fun?
- Is the evidence distinctive, the Arabic natural, and the answer singular?
- Does the concept add a recognition action not already overused?

## 16. Reviewer Checklist

- Route any knowledge, voice, event, power, place, opening, or object-led item.
- Verify cue fairness, accepted names, spoiler tier, and non-repetition.
- Reject concepts that work only because the answer is implied by metadata.

## 17. Asset Curator Checklist

- Inspect the player-facing crop at delivery size with metadata hidden.
- Confirm the intended character is dominant and distinguishable.
- Remove textual leaks without removing the evidence needed to recognize them.

## 18. QA Checklist

- Confirm Challenge and Scope ownership, stable IDs, and one answer.
- Recheck media evidence, leakage, Arabic aliases, spoilers, and batch variety.
- Block unavailable, ambiguous, mislabeled, or near-duplicate assets.

## 19. Good Conceptual Examples

A silhouette whose hair and stance are iconic; distinguishing two characters
through facial details; recognizing a character across clearly established
visual eras. These are directions, not production questions.

## 20. Weak or Misplaced Conceptual Examples

A tiny face at the edge of a battle frame; identifying a speaker from audio;
naming the attack being performed; recalling a character's family tree; or
identifying a mask shown alone.
