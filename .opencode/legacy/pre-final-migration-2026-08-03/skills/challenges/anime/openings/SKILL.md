# Challenge Skill: Anime / Openings

## Authority

Follow `.opencode/knowledge/AKWAN-CONTENT-BIBLE.md` and
`.opencode/skills/worlds/anime/SKILL.md`. Use the selected Anime Content Scope
for sequence, broadcast order, credits, and naming. Route overlaps with
`../CHALLENGE-BOUNDARIES.md`.

## 1. Challenge Identity

Openings is recognition and meaningful association of anime opening themes and
sequences. Opening music or opening-specific visuals must create the gameplay.

## 2. Intended Player Experience

Players experience fast musical nostalgia, visual recall, and shared excitement
as they connect an opening excerpt with its anime, sequence, era, or arc.

## 3. Core Source of Fun

Recognizing the identity and memory of an opening through melody, arrangement,
performance, or distinctive opening choreography—not production minutiae.

## 4. Accepted Content

- opening audio, animation, sequence order, anime or arc association;
- visual motifs specific to an opening rather than the main episode;
- performer or official title only when broadly recognizable and enjoyable;
- comparisons between clearly established versions or broadcast sequences.

## 5. Rejected Content

- background score, insert songs, endings, character songs, or trailers;
- character, voice, scene, or lore tasks merely sourced from an opening;
- obscure chart, release, staffing, or production facts;
- clips relying on title cards, lyrics, captions, metadata, or answer text.

## 6. Boundaries With Neighboring Challenges

- **Characters:** a character shown in opening footage belongs there if visual
  identity is the whole task; opening recognition stays here.
- **Abilities:** a power montage belongs there only when the technique is the
  central object rather than the sequence.
- **Voices:** spoken performance belongs there; sung opening performance is here.
- **Scenes:** canonical events belong there; stylized opening sequences are here.
- **Places:** recognizing a location belongs there unless it is only a cue to
  identify the opening.
- **Symbols and Objects:** object identity belongs there; opening motif identity
  stays here.
- **Otaku:** production facts belong there only when genuinely meaningful;
  opening audio/visual association belongs here.

## 7. Supported Question Formats

Audio recognition, visual-sequence recognition, anime/arc association,
sequence-order distinction, excerpt continuation, and paired opening
comparison. Ownership remains opening-led regardless of format.

## 8. Suitable Media

Short clean audio, non-leaking opening footage, cropped motif stills, or ordered
opening frames. Choose a segment with recognizable evidence but without title,
credit, or lyric disclosure.

## 9. Leakage Risks

Anime title cards, opening title, performer credit, lyrics containing proper
names, subtitles, platform UI, watermark, filename, thumbnail, and metadata can
give away the answer. Visuals may also reveal the exact arc through spoilers.

## 10. Answer Requirements

Declare whether the answer is anime, opening sequence, official song title,
performer, or arc association. Verify numbering across alternate cuts and
regional releases; record canonical and accepted Arabic/romanized forms.

## 11. Arabic and Naming Rules

Use natural Arabic while preserving official song and performer names. Treat
transliteration differences as variants only when they denote the same work;
do not translate titles into unofficial clues.

## 12. Spoiler Policy

Opening visuals can reveal new cast, forms, alliances, deaths, and arc settings.
Use an excerpt safe for the manifest tier, and inspect every frame rather than
assuming an official opening is spoiler-free.

## 13. Variety Expectations

Vary audio and visual recognition, eras, moods, sequence positions, series,
performers, and association actions. Avoid a batch composed only of first
openings, title recall, or the most famous chorus.

## 14. Common Failure Modes

Title card visible; lyrics or subtitles disclose answer; ending mislabeled as
opening; clip too generic; sequence numbering disputed; visual spoiler ignored;
performer trivia too obscure; and music used only as decoration.

## 15. Writer Checklist

- Is opening recognition or association the primary mechanic and fun?
- Is the exact answer type and version unambiguous?
- Are excerpt, leakage, lyric, naming, and spoiler constraints explicit?

## 16. Reviewer Checklist

- Verify the media is an opening and the version/order is supported.
- Route voice, character, ability, scene, place, object, or broad knowledge tasks.
- Reject obscure credits, generic excerpts, textual leaks, and disputed labels.

## 17. Asset Curator Checklist

- Inspect audio, every frame, captions, UI, thumbnail, and delivery metadata.
- Select a recognizable excerpt without titles, credits, or answer-bearing lyrics.
- Verify exact timestamps and fallback availability.

## 18. QA Checklist

- Recheck version, sequence label, answer aliases, spoilers, rights notes, and leaks.
- Test excerpt recognizability at actual duration and playback quality.
- Verify format, era, series, and audio/visual variety.

## 19. Good Conceptual Examples

Recognizing an anime from a distinctive instrumental passage; associating an
opening sequence with its established arc; distinguishing two official opening
versions by choreography. These are directions, not production questions.

## 20. Weak or Misplaced Conceptual Examples

A visible anime logo; naming a character in the sequence; an ending theme;
identifying a spoken actor; obscure sales data; or a lyric that contains the
requested title.
