# Challenge Skill: Anime / Scenes

## Authority

Follow `.opencode/knowledge/AKWAN-CONTENT-BIBLE.md` and
`.opencode/skills/worlds/anime/SKILL.md`. Use the selected Anime Content Scope
for event context and canon. Route overlaps with
`../CHALLENGE-BOUNDARIES.md`.

## 1. Challenge Identity

Scenes is recognition or understanding of a specific anime event through a
still, clip, sequence, or tightly constrained description. The meaningful
moment and what occurs within it are central.

## 2. Intended Player Experience

Players relive confrontations, turning points, emotional beats, reveals, and
consequences, then share the memory or deduction behind the moment.

## 3. Core Source of Fun

Event memory and visual storytelling: recognizing what happened, its immediate
context, or a fair before/after relationship.

## 4. Accepted Content

- confrontations, turning points, reveals, decisions, reunions, and outcomes;
- event identity from a distinctive frame or short coherent clip;
- immediate cause, consequence, before, or after when shown evidence supports it;
- emotional or narrative context that is specific and canonically settled.

## 5. Rejected Content

- simple character, place, power, voice, opening, or object identification;
- broad lore recall not anchored to one event;
- generic action, reaction faces, montage fragments, or contextless frames;
- moments answerable only through subtitles, narration, filename, or synopsis.

## 6. Boundaries With Neighboring Challenges

- **Characters:** identifying who is pictured belongs there; identifying the
  moment involving them belongs here.
- **Abilities:** recognizing the technique belongs there; recognizing the event
  or consequence belongs here.
- **Voices:** identifying a speaker from sound belongs there; dialogue used to
  place the event belongs here only if it does not reveal the answer.
- **Openings:** sequence or theme recognition belongs to Openings.
- **Places:** identifying the setting belongs there; interpreting what happened
  at it belongs here.
- **Symbols and Objects:** identifying an item belongs there; its decisive use
  within an event can belong here.
- **Otaku:** broad history and lore belong there; a bounded depicted moment is
  Scenes.

## 7. Supported Question Formats

Moment recognition, immediate before/after, consequence deduction, event
ordering, missing beat, clip interruption, and constrained scene context.
Format serves event understanding rather than defining the Challenge.

## 8. Suitable Media

Distinctive stills, short coherent clips, small ordered frame sets, or precise
non-leaking descriptions. Media should preserve enough characters, action, and
setting to establish the event fairly.

## 9. Leakage Risks

Subtitles, dialogue, episode titles, recap text, interface labels, captions,
filename, preview frames, and thumbnails may state the event or outcome. A clip
can also accidentally expose what the player is meant to predict.

## 10. Answer Requirements

Define one bounded event, action, cause, or consequence. Specify temporal and
continuity context when needed, accept genuinely equivalent Arabic phrasing,
and reject prompts with several plausible adjacent moments.

## 11. Arabic and Naming Rules

Use concise conversational Arabic and established names. Describe actions
clearly without translating dialogue into an answer clue. Verify event labels
and avoid invented fan titles unless recorded only as accepted aliases.

## 12. Spoiler Policy

Scenes carry high spoiler risk. Match the manifest's anime/manga and recency
tier; consider the still, clip endpoint, dialogue, answer, explanation, and even
the fact that a character appears.

## 13. Variety Expectations

Balance action, emotion, comedy, decisions, reveals, consequences, and
before/after reasoning across Scopes and eras. Avoid only fights, deaths, or
finale moments.

## 14. Common Failure Modes

Contextless frame; subtitle gives answer; clip runs past the asked moment;
question merely names a visible person; event too broad; multiple adjacent
answers; late spoiler in thumbnail; and repetitive fight-outcome recall.

## 15. Writer Checklist

- Is a specific event the primary solving mechanic and emotional payoff?
- Is the requested moment bounded, singular, supported, and tier-safe?
- Does the media requirement preserve context without revealing the response?

## 16. Reviewer Checklist

- Route identity, ability, voice, opening, place, object, or broad lore tasks.
- Verify chronology, immediate context, one answer, Arabic clarity, and fun.
- Reject contextless, overbroad, leaking, or spoiler-mismatched moments.

## 17. Asset Curator Checklist

- Inspect the whole interval, audio, subtitles, thumbnail, and end frame.
- Confirm the event is recognizable from player-facing evidence alone.
- Crop or trim leaks only if event context remains fair and coherent.

## 18. QA Checklist

- Recheck clip boundaries, event identity, answer variants, spoilers, and leaks.
- Confirm Challenge/Scope ownership and source traceability.
- Evaluate batch-level event, tone, era, and format variety.

## 19. Good Conceptual Examples

Recognizing a turning point from a distinctive still; reconstructing the next
immediate beat; connecting a shown decision to its direct consequence. These
are directions, not production questions.

## 20. Weak or Misplaced Conceptual Examples

Naming a visible character; naming an attack; identifying the background city;
a subtitle that states what happened; or recalling an entire arc from one
generic reaction frame.
