# Challenge Skill: Anime / Abilities

## Authority

Follow `.opencode/knowledge/AKWAN-CONTENT-BIBLE.md` and
`.opencode/skills/worlds/anime/SKILL.md`. Use the selected Anime Content Scope
for canon, mechanics, and naming. Route overlaps with
`../CHALLENGE-BOUNDARIES.md`.

## 1. Challenge Identity

Abilities is recognition or understanding of powers, techniques,
transformations, and power systems. The ability or its rule is the primary
object of play.

## 2. Intended Player Experience

Players should feel the excitement of recognizing a signature technique or
working out how a familiar system, cost, limit, effect, or transformation works.

## 3. Core Source of Fun

Power literacy: connecting a distinctive effect to its name, user, function,
constraint, counter, or system rule.

## 4. Accepted Content

- named techniques, powers, transformations, stages, and power systems;
- users or effects when the ability remains the central signal;
- costs, limits, activation conditions, interactions, and counters;
- clear visual or audio manifestations unique enough to identify the ability.

## 5. Rejected Content

- general character identity, biography, lore, or event recall;
- flashy combat with no identifiable technique;
- weapons or artifacts when object recognition is the actual task;
- terminology with disputed meaning, weak evidence, or metadata-only support.

## 6. Boundaries With Neighboring Challenges

- **Characters:** identity from appearance belongs there; a form belongs here
  when its mechanics or power identity are tested.
- **Otaku:** general lore and relationships belong there; system rules stay
  here when ability reasoning creates the fun.
- **Voices:** a vocal performance belongs there unless sound uniquely signals
  activation and the answer is the ability, not the speaker.
- **Scenes:** event recognition belongs there; isolate the technique here.
- **Openings:** opening-sequence recognition belongs to Openings.
- **Places:** environmental recognition belongs to Places, even if altered by
  a power.
- **Symbols and Objects:** name the device or weapon there; name or reason about
  the power it produces here.

## 7. Supported Question Formats

Effect recognition, technique distinction, user-to-power association,
rule/limit deduction, transformation-stage ordering, counter matching, and
short activation-clip recognition. Format never overrides the core mechanic.

## 8. Suitable Media

Focused anime frames, short clips centered on activation or effect, clean
effect audio, diagrams grounded in canon, or minimal structured comparisons.
The ability must be visually or audibly central.

## 9. Leakage Risks

Called-out technique names, subtitles, HUD labels, episode titles, captions,
filename, or narration can state the answer. An iconic user may also collapse
an ability question into character recognition.

## 10. Answer Requirements

Use the canonical ability or rule, with precise scope and verified aliases.
Separate parent systems, individual techniques, transformations, and localized
names. State version or stage when it changes the answer.

## 11. Arabic and Naming Rules

Use clear Arabic explanation while preserving established technique names.
Record Arabic, English, and romanized aliases only after verifying equivalence;
do not merge fan translations that name different levels or moves.

## 12. Spoiler Policy

Late awakenings, forms, users, limits, counters, and explanations may spoil
major developments. Match prompt, media, answer variants, and explanation to
the manifest's anime/manga and recency tier.

## 13. Variety Expectations

Balance recognition, rules, limits, users, effects, counters, systems, and
transformations across Scopes. Avoid a batch made only of attack names or the
strongest characters.

## 14. Common Failure Modes

Generic explosions; answer shouted in audio; confusing a weapon with its
power; relying on the visible user; accepting loose translations; testing raw
power rankings; and using an event whose technique is not isolated.

## 15. Writer Checklist

- Does ability knowledge, not character or scene recall, solve the item?
- Is the mechanic canonical, bounded, and supported by the selected Scope?
- Are aliases, version, media requirement, and spoiler tier explicit?

## 16. Reviewer Checklist

- Verify system-versus-technique precision and one intended answer.
- Route identity, event, object, place, voice, opening, or broad lore items.
- Reject generic effects, ranking debates, leaks, and unsupported mechanics.

## 17. Asset Curator Checklist

- Inspect only the delivered media and verify the ability is central.
- Mute or crop spoken/written names while retaining sufficient effect evidence.
- Reject visually impressive combat that does not identify the intended power.

## 18. QA Checklist

- Recheck canon, translations, versions, spoilers, and answer uniqueness.
- Confirm media evidence and leakage at actual playback conditions.
- Check batch balance and block near-duplicate techniques or repeated actions.

## 19. Good Conceptual Examples

Recognizing a technique from its distinctive activation; connecting a known
limitation to its system; distinguishing two related transformation stages.
These are directions, not production questions.

## 20. Weak or Misplaced Conceptual Examples

A generic battle flash; naming the fighter shown; recalling who won the fight;
identifying a sword with no power mechanic; or ranking abilities by fan opinion.
