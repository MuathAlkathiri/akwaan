# Challenge Skill: Anime / Places

## Authority

Follow `.opencode/knowledge/AKWAN-CONTENT-BIBLE.md` and
`.opencode/skills/worlds/anime/SKILL.md`. Use the selected Anime Content Scope
for geography, canon, and naming. Route overlaps with
`../CHALLENGE-BOUNDARIES.md`.

## 1. Challenge Identity

Places is recognition or spatial understanding of anime locations and settings.
The environment must supply the primary evidence and location identity must be
the object of play.

## 2. Intended Player Experience

Players feel transported into familiar worlds, noticing architecture,
landscape, atmosphere, layout, and landmarks that make a location memorable.

## 3. Core Source of Fun

Environmental recognition and spatial memory—not identifying the character,
event, symbol, or lore associated with the background.

## 4. Accepted Content

- cities, islands, villages, schools, arenas, bases, realms, and interiors;
- distinctive architecture, geography, landmarks, layout, or atmosphere;
- spatial relations or travel context when canonical and reasonably memorable;
- environmental comparisons with one clear location answer.

## 5. Rejected Content

- generic forests, rooms, skies, streets, or battlefields;
- backgrounds where a character or event provides the real clue;
- detached building symbols, flags, vehicles, or objects;
- geography trivia, maps, or lore unsupported by a stable canonical source.

## 6. Boundaries With Neighboring Challenges

- **Characters:** identifying the foreground person belongs there; the setting
  must independently identify the answer here.
- **Abilities:** recognizing a power-shaped environment belongs there when the
  effect is the main signal.
- **Voices:** voice recognition belongs there; environmental ambience can
  support Places only when location identity remains central.
- **Scenes:** identifying what happened belongs there; identifying where from
  environmental cues belongs here.
- **Openings:** opening-sequence recognition belongs there even if it depicts a
  landmark.
- **Symbols and Objects:** a single sign, vehicle, ship, emblem, or artifact
  belongs there unless it is subordinate to a recognizable environment.
- **Otaku:** geographic lore belongs there when recall, not environment or
  spatial reasoning, solves it.

## 7. Supported Question Formats

Environment recognition, landmark crop, panoramic reveal, interior/exterior
matching, route or spatial relation, location distinction, and constrained map
interpretation. Media type alone never establishes ownership.

## 8. Suitable Media

Wide or medium environmental stills, short establishing shots, safe maps,
panoramas, or spatial diagrams. The location cues must remain clear at display
size without depending on labels.

## 9. Leakage Risks

Signs, maps, captions, subtitles, station names, storefronts, filenames,
watermarks, or spoken dialogue may state the location. Foreground characters or
famous events can also bypass environmental reasoning.

## 10. Answer Requirements

Use the canonical location at the requested granularity. Do not accept a
country, city, building, and room interchangeably. Verify translated names,
aliases, continuity, and any temporal changes.

## 11. Arabic and Naming Rules

Use concise Arabic and established geographic names. Record faithful Arabic,
English, and romanized variants; distinguish homonyms and nested locations
without overloading the prompt.

## 12. Spoiler Policy

New realms, destroyed locations, altered landmarks, occupants, flags, and
travel routes can reveal later developments. Match asset, answer, and
explanation to the manifest tier.

## 13. Variety Expectations

Vary exterior/interior, natural/built, scale, atmosphere, spatial reasoning,
eras, and selected Scopes. Avoid a batch dominated by headquarters, maps, or
one famous city.

## 14. Common Failure Modes

Generic background; tiny location; foreground character gives it away; label
states answer; granularity mismatch; map not canonical; event recognition
masquerades as place; and overly obscure one-use settings.

## 15. Writer Checklist

- Does the environment independently provide the primary solving signal?
- Is the requested geographic granularity exact and singular?
- Are naming, continuity, spoiler, and media requirements documented?

## 16. Reviewer Checklist

- Hide foreground identity and event context; test whether place still leads.
- Route ability, voice, opening, scene, object, character, or lore-led concepts.
- Reject generic, labeled, disputed, or excessively obscure settings.

## 17. Asset Curator Checklist

- Inspect at delivery size and ensure architectural or landscape cues survive.
- Remove labels and leaks without destroying location evidence.
- Avoid frames whose character or event overwhelms the environment.

## 18. QA Checklist

- Confirm granularity, aliases, canon, continuity, spoilers, and evidence.
- Verify the actual player-facing asset is clear and leakage-safe.
- Check setting, scale, Scope, era, and format variety.

## 19. Good Conceptual Examples

Recognizing a city by its skyline; matching a distinctive interior to its
location; using canonical spatial cues to distinguish neighboring places. These
are directions, not production questions.

## 20. Weak or Misplaced Conceptual Examples

A generic forest; a battle frame identified by its fighters; a labeled map;
naming a flag; recalling who governs an island; or identifying an opening from
its landmark montage.
