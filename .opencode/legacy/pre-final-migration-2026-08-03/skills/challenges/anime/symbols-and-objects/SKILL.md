# Challenge Skill: Anime / Symbols and Objects

## Authority

Follow `.opencode/knowledge/AKWAN-CONTENT-BIBLE.md` and
`.opencode/skills/worlds/anime/SKILL.md`. Use the selected Anime Content Scope
for object identity, canon, and naming. Route overlaps with
`../CHALLENGE-BOUNDARIES.md`.

## 1. Challenge Identity

Symbols and Objects is recognition and meaningful association of distinctive
weapons, masks, emblems, flags, uniforms, ships, devices, artifacts, clothing,
and other designed things. The item itself is central.

## 2. Intended Player Experience

Players notice iconic details, connect a designed thing to its role or owner,
and enjoy the tactile visual memory of objects that carry story identity.

## 3. Core Source of Fun

Object literacy: recognizing form, markings, construction, use, or symbolic
meaning from the thing itself—not from a full character or surrounding event.

## 4. Accepted Content

- distinctive weapons, masks, emblems, flags, uniforms, ships, and vehicles;
- devices, artifacts, clothing, tools, tokens, and recurring story objects;
- owner, faction, function, or meaning when the item supplies the main evidence;
- detail or usage comparisons between clearly distinguishable objects.

## 5. Rejected Content

- generic props with no memorable identity;
- full character images where the person supplies the answer;
- power effects, complete events, locations, or lore with only incidental objects;
- hidden, tiny, ambiguous, misrepresented, or metadata-dependent items.

## 6. Boundaries With Neighboring Challenges

- **Characters:** a worn cue may support identity there; isolate and recognize
  the mask, clothing, or item itself here.
- **Abilities:** a weapon/device is here; its power mechanic is Abilities when
  that mechanic creates the challenge.
- **Voices:** human vocal performance belongs there; an object's sound may
  support this Challenge only when object identity is central.
- **Scenes:** the decisive event belongs there; the recognizable thing used in
  it belongs here.
- **Openings:** opening motifs belong there when sequence recognition is primary.
- **Places:** a ship or building-like designed object is here when treated as an
  object; a navigable environment or spatial setting belongs to Places.
- **Otaku:** history, ownership facts, or symbolism belong there when recall,
  rather than recognition of the item, is the actual mechanic.

## 7. Supported Question Formats

Close-up recognition, silhouette, detail-to-whole, object-owner or faction
association, function distinction, matching, progressive reveal, and short use
clip. Format serves item recognition and does not define ownership alone.

## 8. Suitable Media

Clean close-ups, isolated models, readable emblems, short clips of handling or
use, silhouettes, or carefully framed contextual stills. The actual item must
be visible and distinguishable at delivery size.

## 9. Leakage Risks

Engraved names, flag text, interface labels, subtitles, captions, packaging,
filename, metadata, visible owner, or scene context can disclose the answer.
Over-cropping can remove the very details that make the item identifiable.

## 10. Answer Requirements

Use the canonical object, symbol, class, or owner association at explicit
granularity. Verify variants, upgrades, replicas, inherited objects, and
translated names; reject visually identical candidates without a fair separator.

## 11. Arabic and Naming Rules

Use natural Arabic and established fandom terms. Preserve proper names where
translation would be misleading, record verified transliterations, and
distinguish an object's formal name from its generic type.

## 12. Spoiler Policy

Later weapons, masks, flags, uniforms, ships, ownership changes, damage, and
upgrades may expose major reveals. Match crop, answer, variants, and explanation
to the manifest tier.

## 13. Variety Expectations

Vary object classes, scales, materials, functions, owners, factions, close-ups,
and association actions across Scopes. Avoid a batch made only of weapons,
logos, or protagonist belongings.

## 14. Common Failure Modes

Generic prop; object tiny or occluded; owner gives answer; symbol unreadable;
power effect dominates; function inferred only from lore; wrong granularity;
replica confused with original; and repeated weapon silhouettes.

## 15. Writer Checklist

- Is the item itself the primary evidence and source of fun?
- Is identity or association precise, canonical, and answerable once?
- Are required details, context limits, naming, and spoiler tier explicit?

## 16. Reviewer Checklist

- Route person, power, voice, event, opening, place, or lore-led concepts.
- Verify distinctiveness, granularity, ownership, variants, and one answer.
- Reject generic, tiny, hidden, leaking, or metadata-dependent objects.

## 17. Asset Curator Checklist

- Conduct a metadata-blind inspection at actual display size.
- Confirm the intended item is visible, dominant, and distinguishable.
- Remove owner/text leaks only when enough object evidence remains.

## 18. QA Checklist

- Recheck identity, aliases, variants, spoilers, leakage, and asset availability.
- Confirm Scope and Challenge ownership and evidence in the delivered media.
- Check class, owner, scale, Scope, and interaction variety.

## 19. Good Conceptual Examples

Recognizing an iconic mask from its design; matching a faction emblem; identifying
a ship from its silhouette; distinguishing two related artifacts by construction.
These are directions, not production questions.

## 20. Weak or Misplaced Conceptual Examples

A sword hidden behind its wielder; a generic cup; naming a character from their
uniform; identifying an attack effect; recalling an artifact's full history; or
naming a city from its flag alone.
