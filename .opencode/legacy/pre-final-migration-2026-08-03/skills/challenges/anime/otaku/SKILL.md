# Challenge Skill: Anime / Otaku

## Authority

Follow `.opencode/knowledge/AKWAN-CONTENT-BIBLE.md` and
`.opencode/skills/worlds/anime/SKILL.md`. Use the selected Anime Content Scope
for canon and naming. Route overlaps with `../CHALLENGE-BOUNDARIES.md`.

## 1. Challenge Identity

Otaku is Anime's general-knowledge Challenge. Its core experience is the
satisfying recall or deduction of meaningful knowledge about a selected work.
It is broad in subject matter but precise in gameplay: the fun must come from
knowing the world, history, relationships, or context—not from recognizing a
piece of media owned by another Challenge.

## 2. Intended Player Experience

Otaku should feel like an animated conversation among fans. Players connect
facts, remember consequences, compare eras, and rediscover lore they care about.
The reveal should invite recognition or discussion rather than reward arbitrary
memorization.

Otaku provides a home for strong general knowledge that does not fit a more
specific Anime Challenge. It is not a catch-all for leftovers.

## 3. Core Source of Fun

Meaningful fan recall and deduction: connecting lore, relationships, history,
institutions, chronology, or world rules in a way that rewards understanding
and creates a satisfying reveal or conversation.

## 4. Accepted Content

- organizations, alliances, crews, factions, and meaningful membership;
- relationships when their significance or change is the knowledge tested;
- major events, causes, consequences, and stable chronology;
- lore, world rules, social structures, titles, and institutions;
- creators only when the fact is genuinely interesting to fans;
- memorable facts with a clear story or world connection;
- unambiguous comparisons across established eras;
- broad knowledge whose gameplay is recall, connection, or deduction.

## 7. Supported Question Formats

- connect an organization to its purpose or place in the world;
- recall a consequence of a major decision or event;
- distinguish two easily confused institutions or historical periods;
- identify the stable rule that explains a familiar outcome;
- place major developments in an unambiguous order;
- recall a meaningful title, affiliation, promise, or world concept;
- reveal an interesting creation fact that fans would enjoy discussing.

These are concept directions, not production questions.

## 6. Boundaries With Neighboring Challenges

Otaku must not absorb content whose core fun belongs to:

- Characters: identifying or naming a character;
- Abilities: recognizing, naming, or explaining a power as the primary action;
- Openings: recognizing music, sequence, or opening identity;
- Voices: identifying a speaker or performer from audio;
- Scenes: recognizing or observing a specific scene or clip;
- Places: identifying a location from visual or spatial evidence;
- Symbols or Objects: identifying a flag, emblem, item, weapon, or artifact.

If removing the recognition media leaves no meaningful knowledge challenge, the
item belongs elsewhere. If the prompt merely asks for a name from an image,
sound, or clip, it is not Otaku.

## 5. Rejected Content

- isolated dates, counts, birthdays, measurements, rankings, and production
  credits without genuine discussion value;
- facts supported only by fan assumptions, mistranslations, or promotional copy;
- questions that require current information but were not freshly verified;
- disputed timelines presented as settled;
- trivia whose answer is visible or stated in the prompt;
- broad prompts with several defensible answers;
- content selected because it does not fit anywhere else;
- manga-only revelations in an anime-safe batch.

## 13. Variety Expectations

A batch should vary organizations, eras, world concepts, event consequences,
relationships in context, chronology, and lore. It should not repeatedly ask
for memberships, titles, or numerical facts. Coverage should reflect the
selected Scopes and spoiler policy rather than concentrating on the latest or
most famous arc.

Variety must remain Challenge-consistent: different subjects, same clear promise
of meaningful fan knowledge.

## 8. Suitable Media

Otaku is naturally compatible with text and structured content. Media is used
only when it supplies evidence for a knowledge action, comparison, or deduction
without turning the item into another Challenge. Decorative images are excluded.

Media must be answer-bearing without being answer-giving. Check subtitles,
labels, interface text, captions, filenames, spoken answers, and contextual
giveaways. An attractive asset cannot justify moving a weak concept into Otaku.

## 9. Leakage Risks

Subtitles, labels, narration, dialogue, captions, filenames, metadata, timelines,
or diagrams can state the answer. Remove those leaks only when the remaining
player-facing evidence still supports the intended reasoning.

## 10. Answer Requirements

- one intended answer or a deliberately closed set;
- canonical internal form with verified Arabic and English aliases;
- no acceptance of broader or merely related concepts;
- explicit temporal context for changing affiliations, titles, or institutions;
- explanation must show why the answer follows without introducing a new
  unsupported claim;
- contested translations must be acknowledged through accepted variants or a
  safer concept.

## 12. Spoiler Policy

Follow the manifest's spoiler policy and the Scope's tiering. Anime-safe means
anime-released knowledge only; manga knowledge never leaks through explanations,
accepted variants, or media.

Reject or return any item with unclear continuity, conflicting official
translations, multiple valid periods, disputed canon, or an unstated version.
Repair by adding fair temporal or continuity context only when that context does
not reveal the answer.

## 11. Arabic and Naming Rules

- use conversational Modern Arabic suitable for a group game;
- keep prompts short and direct;
- use established fandom names consistently;
- avoid translation-shaped grammar and excessive English mixing;
- do not embed the answer through a literal definition or transliteration clue;
- ensure accepted Arabic spellings refer to the exact intended entity.

## 14. Common Failure Modes

Catch-all routing; arbitrary statistics; biography used as character identity;
media recognition disguised as knowledge; several defensible answers; disputed
canon presented as settled; stale time-sensitive facts; and trivia with no
social or narrative payoff.

## 15. Writer Checklist

- Does meaningful knowledge or deduction create the fun?
- Is the fact supported, stable, Scope-correct, and worth discussing?
- Are one intended answer, variants, temporal context, and spoiler tier explicit?
- Does the concept add subject and reasoning variety without leaving Otaku?

## 16. Reviewer Checklist

- Apply every boundary above and recommend the dedicated Challenge when needed.
- Verify traceability, ambiguity, Arabic, answer uniqueness, and social value.
- Reject unsupported, arbitrary, leaking, repetitive, or spoiler-unsafe trivia.

## 17. Asset Curator Checklist

- Use media only when it contributes evidence to the knowledge mechanic.
- Inspect the delivered asset with filename and metadata hidden.
- Confirm evidence sufficiency and remove written, spoken, or contextual leaks.
- Reject decorative media and assets that turn the concept into recognition.

## 18. QA Checklist

- Confirm Product Bible, Anime World, Challenge, Scope, and spoiler alignment.
- Recheck research traceability, answers, variants, Arabic, and media evidence.
- Audit duplicates and coverage of lore, eras, relationships, rules, and actions.
- Block any item whose true solving mechanic belongs to another Challenge.

## 19. Good Conceptual Examples

- a meaningful distinction between two major institutions;
- the consequence that changed an alliance or era;
- an established world rule applied to a familiar situation;
- a stable ordering of major developments;
- an interesting creator choice with clear fan relevance.

## 20. Weak or Misplaced Conceptual Examples

- a character portrait followed by a name request;
- a technique clip followed by an ability-name request;
- an opening excerpt followed by its number or title;
- a frame whose main challenge is identifying the location;
- an obscure publication statistic with no story or discussion value;
- a disputed fan theory presented as canon.

## Pack-Level Review Prompts

- Does knowledge, rather than media recognition, create the fun?
- Does the item belong to Otaku rather than a dedicated Anime Challenge?
- Is it inside the selected Scope and spoiler policy?
- Is the fact supported, stable, and worth discussing?
- Is there exactly one intended answer?
- Are temporal and translation ambiguities resolved?
- Is the Arabic natural and non-revealing?
- Is any media necessary, sufficient, and leakage-safe?
- Does the batch vary subjects and eras without losing Challenge identity?
- Would a real group enjoy the reveal?
