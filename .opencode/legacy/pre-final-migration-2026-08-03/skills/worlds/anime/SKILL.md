# World Skill: Anime

## Identity

Anime is a World: a complete social experience built around stories, emotion,
recognition, fandom memory, and shared cultural knowledge. It is not a generic
content category.

Works such as One Piece, Naruto, and Attack on Titan are Scopes.
ChallengeTypes belong to the Anime World. ContentItems belong to exactly one
selected Scope and may be consumed only by compatible ChallengeTypes.

## Intended Audience

Anime serves mixed groups ranging from casual viewers to dedicated fans. A
Challenge should make its expected familiarity understandable through its
identity and content choices rather than requiring players to configure
expertise. The experience should reward attention, affection, and shared memory,
not encyclopedic recall for its own sake.

## Emotional Experience

Anime play should evoke recognition, nostalgia, surprise, excitement, friendly
debate, and the pleasure of remembering a detail just before the reveal.
Questions should connect to why audiences care: adventures, relationships,
turning points, rivalries, humor, world rules, mysteries, sacrifices, and iconic
moments.

## Suitable Media

Anime may use authentic episode frames, bounded clips, dialogue or sound,
opening and ending material, symbols, maps, structured sequences, and carefully
selected text. Media is first-class only when it creates the Challenge action.
It must contain usable evidence, remain clear, and avoid titles, subtitles,
labels, or spoken lines that expose the answer.

Media belongs only to the ContentItem. A general-knowledge ChallengeType should
not become scene recognition merely because a screenshot is available.

## Arabic Naming and Wording

- Write player-facing Arabic naturally and concisely.
- Preserve established names when Arabic fandom usage is stable.
- Keep an official English or romanized form as an accepted variant when useful.
- Avoid literal translations that Arabic audiences do not use.
- Do not mix multiple transliteration systems inside one batch.
- Use Arabic punctuation and readable sentence structure.
- Prefer character and in-world names over performer names unless the Challenge
  explicitly makes creation or performance the source of fun.

## Localization and Romanization

Keep one canonical internal form for each named entity, plus documented Arabic,
English, and common romanized aliases. Where official English localization and
Japanese romanization differ, select the form best established for the target
audience and accept the other verified form. Never infer spelling from sound
alone when authoritative naming is available.

Translation-sensitive titles, groups, techniques, and locations must be marked
in Scope knowledge. Accepted variants must refer to the same concept, not merely
something related.

## Spoiler Handling

Every batch manifest defines a spoiler policy. Respect the boundary across
research, wording, answers, explanations, and media.

- Material released in the anime is distinct from manga-only material.
- Recent anime developments may require an additional cooling period.
- A reveal can remain a spoiler even when its name is widely searchable.
- Media thumbnails, captions, and explanations must follow the same boundary as
  the question.
- When release status differs by region or platform, use the most conservative
  boundary or report the ambiguity.

## What Makes Anime Content Enjoyable

- the core memory is meaningful to viewers;
- the Challenge action is immediately understandable;
- the answer creates recognition or a satisfying reveal;
- the content respects the selected work and its audience;
- variety comes from different moments and kinds of thinking while remaining
  native to the Challenge;
- demand feels fair and arises from familiarity or detail, not obscurity; no
  difficulty tier is stored on the ContentItem.

## Common Failure Modes

- isolated production facts with no social or emotional payoff;
- direct identity prompts better owned by another Challenge;
- mixing anime and manga continuity without disclosure;
- unmarked spoilers;
- inconsistent Arabic names or overly literal localization;
- ambiguous timelines, affiliations, transformations, or temporary states;
- decorative media;
- repeated focus on protagonists or one famous arc;
- forcing every Anime Challenge to use the same gameplay.

## Scope Boundaries

A Content Scope defines the work or deliberately bounded franchise material.
Crossovers, adaptations, films, specials, games, novels, live action, and
non-canonical material are outside the default boundary unless the manifest and
Scope knowledge explicitly include them. Anime-only and manga-only facts must
remain labeled.

## Challenge Ownership

Anime owns its Challenge set. Each Challenge defines what players do and what
content belongs there. Do not route by whichever question wording is easiest.
When the core fun is identifying a character, ability, opening, voice, scene,
place, symbol, or object, use that dedicated Challenge. General knowledge belongs
to Otaku only when knowledge itself is the enjoyable action.
